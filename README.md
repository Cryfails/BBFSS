Lantern Engine runs your own copy of Bloodborne on Windows and Linux: a launcher and my engine, built on bbport and shadPS4 and reworked by me for performance. The game's own code runs natively on your CPU; its graphics are translated to Vulkan.

**No game files are included.** You need your own legal copy of Bloodborne (CUSA03173) with update 1.09, dumped from your own PS4.

**Help:** [Help & FAQ](https://awakenchan.github.io/LanternEngine/): setup, a graphics settings guide, fixes for common problems and the beta guide. Bug reports go to the `#issues` forum on the [Discord](https://discord.gg/d3Uv3Cfr5x).

## Install

1. Download `LanternEngine-<version>-win64.zip` from [Releases](https://github.com/Awakenchan/LanternEngine/releases) and extract it anywhere.
2. Run `Lantern Engine.exe` and pick your game folder (the one with `eboot.bin`).
3. Press **Play**. The launcher updates itself. `Bloodborne.exe` starts the game directly (add it to Steam as a non-Steam game).

### Linux (beta)

1. Download `LanternEngine-<version>-linux-x64.tar.gz` from [Releases](https://github.com/Awakenchan/LanternEngine/releases) and unpack it (`tar xzf LanternEngine-*-linux-x64.tar.gz`).
2. Run `Lantern Engine` in the unpacked folder, pick your game folder and press **Play**. From a terminal: `./lantern.sh /path/to/CUSA03173`.
3. It needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa or NVIDIA), Python 3 and glibc 2.38 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Fedora 39, Arch, SteamOS 3.6, Bazzite). From 0.9.37 beta 7 the launcher updates itself on Linux too, and DLSS Super Resolution works on RTX cards with NVIDIA's driver (DLSS Frame Generation is Windows only).

## Keys in the game

- **Insert** or **L3 + R3**: the settings menu (with `BB_OVERLAY_KEYS=insert` only Insert opens it, with `off` neither does).
- **F2** or **L3 + D-pad Up**: the co-op menu.
- **F3** or **L3 + D-pad Left**: clean shot for filming. Hides the game's HUD, menus and subtitles in every frame that draws the world (loading screens keep theirs), and the overlay's FPS counter and notes: from the game's first menu-movie (Scaleform) draw onto the finished picture or its clip mask after the scene, to the end of the frame, every draw onto that picture or its clip masks is left out, whatever draws it, the dims and fades drawn there too; a draw onto the picture before that first movie draw stays, and draws into other images are kept. It hides them until pressed again or the settings menu opens. Turning it on closes the co-op menu (F2); when this key turns it off again, the co-op menu opens again if clean shot closed it. While it is on, the log has every 300 frames `Camera: clean shot, last 300 frames: ...`: frames whose UI it found and left out, frames that drew the world with no UI found, frames without the world, the draws left out, and the draws onto the finished picture before the UI (the scene's own late passes count there too, so it is not 0; more of them per frame in some state than when standing is a HUD piece drawn before the first movie draw, which stays). Not saved: off at every start.
- **F4** or **L3 + D-pad Right**: orbit for filming. The game's follow camera turns around the hunter by itself, easing in and out; the game gets no pad input meanwhile, so the hunter stands. While it turns: **Left/Right** or **D-pad Left/Right** change the speed in steps of 3 degrees a second (it starts at 12; 0 holds the camera, below 0 turns the other way, at most 45), **Up/Down** or **D-pad Up/Down** move the camera's pitch in steps of 5 degrees (at most 8 steps either way from where it started, inside the game's own limits), **Page Up/Page Down** or **L1/R1** bring the camera closer or farther (x0.5 to x1.5 of the distance; needs PC Camera Controls on at start, or a screen narrower than 16:9). Each start begins again at 12 degrees a second, the pitch it had and x1.0. It ends when pressed again or the settings menu opens (easing out; moving the stick or the mouse meanwhile gives the camera back at once, and the distance eases back), in the first frame of a loading screen or when a cutscene starts or ends (the distance goes back at once), and when the game turns the camera itself for 30 camera updates in a row, as when locked on (the distance eases back). It does not start while a loading screen is up or when the follow camera has not run in the last 0.25 s. Needs PC controls on. Not saved: off at every start. While it turns, the log has every 300 camera updates `Camera: orbit turning, N degrees in T s so far`. `BB_CAMERA_ORBIT` (below) starts it without a key.
- **F5**: adds the follow camera's pose of the moment as the next key of the camera path (its direction and pitch, and while the orbit runs its distance; at most 32 keys). **Shift+F5** clears the keys. The keys are written to `camera_path.txt` in the engine's user folder at every change and read at the first F5 or F6 of a run, so a path set up at a place plays the same directions there in later runs.
- **F6**: plays the camera path, or stops it. The camera glides from where it is through every key in turn on a smooth curve, `BB_CAMERA_PATH_S` seconds from key to key (4 by default), easing in over the first and out over the last stretch (at most 1.5 s each); between two keys it turns the short way round. The game gets no pad input meanwhile, as with the orbit, so the hunter stands. It ends at the last key (the camera stays there and the distance eases back to normal), when pressed again, with **F4** or the settings menu (the camera stays where it is), in the first frame of a loading screen or when a cutscene starts or ends, and when the game turns the camera itself for 30 camera updates in a row (locked on). It does not start while the orbit runs (`Camera: path not started`), without keys, while a loading screen is up or when the follow camera has not run in the last 0.25 s. The pitch stays inside the game's limits; the distance needs PC Camera Controls on at start, like the orbit's. Needs PC controls on.
- **F8**: freeze for stills. The game and its picture hold (the game is not told that its frame was shown) until **F8** again, the settings menu opens, or `BB_CAMERA_FREEZE_S` seconds pass (60 by default). Nothing holds the sound; the engine's overlay is not redrawn meanwhile. Refused while a co-op party is up or while spectating. Not saved: off at every start.
- A D-pad press that opens or toggles one of these (L3 held when it went down) is not the game's until it is released; other D-pad presses are, also with L3 held (D-pad Down, or a D-pad button that went down before L3).
- **F11**: fullscreen on or off.
- **F9**: starts and stops a pad recording when the game was started with `BB_PAD_RECORD=<file>`.

Camera mode reads three environment variables, each once (no ini key): `BB_CAMERA_ORBIT` at the first frame, the other two when their key is first used; `Camera: path on` shows the seconds a key, `Camera: freeze held` the longest freeze:

| Variable | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `BB_CAMERA_PATH_S` | Seconds a camera path (F6) takes from one key to the next, 0.5 to 60; another value gives the default | `4` |
| `BB_CAMERA_FREEZE_S` | Longest freeze (F8) in seconds, 1 to 600; the freeze then ends by itself; another value gives the default | `60` |
| `BB_CAMERA_ORBIT` | Starts the orbit (F4) by itself once a run, at this speed in degrees a second (clamped to -45 to 45, below 0 the other way round), when the follow camera has run for 2 s with no loading screen up and the settings menu closed; from then on it is F4's orbit (the keys change it; F4, the settings menu, a loading screen or lock-on end it, and it does not start again). For clips and test runs without a key press; with clean shot on (F3) the picture has no HUD. The log says `Camera: BB_CAMERA_ORBIT=...` once (with `unavailable` when PC controls are off), then `Camera: orbit on (BB_CAMERA_ORBIT)`. Unset, `0` or not a number: off | unset |

### Ray-traced lamp shadows

`graphics_lamp_shadow_softness` (`sharp`, `soft` or `softer`, default `soft`) sets how soft ray-traced lamp and torch shadows are, as the lamp's radius: a point, 0.1 m or 0.2 m. Sharp traces every lamp that reaches a pixel and averages nothing; soft and softer average over about ten frames. `graphics_lamp_shadow_half` (default off) traces one pixel of each 2x2 block first and the rest only where the answers around it differ; an object thinner than about 2 pixels can lose its shadow. Both need `graphics_rt_point_shadows`. Both have a row in the launcher and in the in-game menu, which switches them at once, so they are not in the table above.

These environment variables tune the lamp shadow pass. Each is read once when the game starts; a number out of range is clamped and text that is not a number leaves the default. They are for testing and are part of no look.

| Variable | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|

| `BB_LAMP_SHADOW_RADIUS_PER_LAMP` | Each lamp gets a disc of its own size from its reach, in the pass that averages over frames: radius = clamp(value x reach, 0.03 m, 0.3 m) x the softness radius / 0.1 m, at most 0.5 m; a lamp followed from frame to frame moves its disc 1/16 of the way to that each frame, so a flickering flame keeps a steady one (1.6 times larger or smaller: at once); its rays are blind within the larger of `BB_LAMP_SHADOW_CLEARANCE` and that radius + 0.05 m. Sharp has no disc, so it changes nothing there. 0 to 1 per metre of reach; 0 is off (one radius for every lamp); 0.02 keeps a lamp of 5 m reach at the soft radius | `0` |
| `BB_LAMP_SHADOW_FLAME` | How a lamp's light is spread over the disc its shadow rays go to (soft or softer lamps; with `BB_LAMP_SHADOW_RADIUS_PER_LAMP` each lamp's own disc). `1` (flame): half the light in a bright core of half the disc's radius, half in a dim envelope of twice the radius, so a shadow's edge is a little crisper in its middle (20% to 80% of the light across 0.85 radii against the even disc's 0.98) and its faint outer fringe reaches about twice as far (2% to 98%: 3.3 radii against 1.8); like every penumbra both widen with the distance behind the caster. `2` (tall flame): the flame of `1` half as wide as it is tall, so seen from the side the sides of its shadows are about twice as crisp (middle 0.42 radii) and their tops and tips as soft as with `1`. | `BB_LAMP_SHADOW_COLOUR` | `1` (to try): each lamp's share of a shadow keeps the lamp's colour, so where a warm and a cool lamp overlap, the shadow of one is lit in the colour of the other; the lamp light keeps the brightness the grey shadow gives it. ### Interior exposure (in development)

Part of the lamp work: a brighten-only exposure lift, so rooms lit only by lamps (tested with `graphics_disable_player_light=1`) read dim but legible instead of black. **On by default, with the largest lift 0.75 stops** (the owner's pick, 2026-11-16, after seeing the Hunter's Dream house, the sewer, the library and the church at off, +0.75 and +1). The largest lift is the setting `graphics_interior_brightness` (0 to 3 stops, default `0.75`, `0` = off). It has a slider in the in-game menu (**Insert**, section Interior brightness, steps of 0.25) and is read every frame, so a change acts at once. At `0`, with no `BB_INTERIOR_EXPOSURE` set, nothing is made, recorded or printed and the factor is exactly 1: the same cost as the feature off. It lifts only dark views (strength 1; the target is not fixed since r99e: it is 0.02 times 2 to the power of the largest lift, at most 0.25, so it is 0.034 at the default 0.75, 0.08 at 2 and 0.16 at 3, and the slider moves both the cap and how bright a room may be and still be lifted; before r99e the target stayed 0.02 and a room metered above it, the library with the hand lantern out at 0.022 in the owner's build-72 run, got +0.000 EV at every slider position; a view metered at the target or above gets nothing) and none that show 15% or more samples at depth 1 or at the open distance (80 m) or farther. **A second gate (r99d) keeps it out of deep outdoor courtyards**: the graveyard at night, ringed by tall cathedral walls, showed 1% open samples but 64% at 20 m and with the open gate alone got the full +0.75 outdoors. Now the lift also fades with the far share (samples at depth 1 or a quarter of the open distance, 20 m, or farther): full up to 18%, none from 40%, half at 29% (the church's long hall, 11.3%, keeps the full lift; the library and the dream house, 0 to 3%, are unchanged). Sky samples count as open and as far, so a view that is mostly sky gets nothing; a ceiling or vault closer than 20 m with no sky in the view is taken as an interior after about 3 s Measured with the hero light off (2026-11-15; log-average before the tone curve; no measured view had any sample at depth 1), and what the defaults do with it: **without the BB PC Remaster mod the library meters about 0.014 and gets the full +0.75 at the default (+0.58 when it meters 0.022, with the hand lantern out; +0.83, +1.83 and +2.83 at slider values 1, 2 and 3)**; with the mod (0.0011 to 0.0055) it gets the full +0.75. Church-candles (0.040) gets nothing without the mod, with the mod (0.0053 to 0.012) +0.7 to +0.75. The Hunter's Dream house meters 0.018 to 0.025 (0.030 with the mod) and gets +0.75 (0.018) to +0.43 (0.025) at the default, +0.17 at 0.030, nothing from 0.034 up (before r99e: at most +0.15 EV, 0 from 0.02 up; a lower slider value takes it down). The graveyard and other open places get none (open samples; the graveyard at night showed 100% sky). A dark street whose view shows few open samples is lifted like a room (the dark alley, 0.025 without the mod, is above the target: nothing; with the mod, 0.0097: up to +0.75); how many open samples real streets show is not fully measured, the log line measures it. ### Lamp flicker (in development)

Both strengths have a slider in the in-game menu (**Insert**, section Light glow and flicker, changes at once) and in the launcher (Graphics > Lighting).

Candles, lanterns, torches and street gas lamps flicker gently, and their light warms as it dims. On by default for flame lamps the game keeps steady, off for flame lamps the game changes itself: the strengths are the `lantern.ini` keys `graphics_lamp_flicker_strength` (steady lamps, 0 to 1, default `1`) and `graphics_lamp_flicker_on_game_strength` (lamps the game changes itself, 0 to 1, default `0`), taken at the end of every frame for the next one. `BB_LAMP_FLICKER` and `BB_LAMP_FLICKER_ON_GAME`, when set and not empty, win over them. These environment variables are read once, at the end of the first frame; in `BB_LAMP_FLICKER` or `BB_LAMP_FLICKER_ON_GAME`, text that does not begin with a number is a strength of 0 (in `BB_LAMP_FLICKER_TEMP` it gives the default `0.6`). With both strengths at 0 nothing of the flicker runs; when a strength goes above 0 again after that, every lamp starts as a new one (it waits 1 s, then its flicker fades in over 1 s). The log has a `Lamp flicker (r109a):` line with both strengths and where each came from (setting or environment) at the end of the first frame, and again after a change, at most once every 600 frames. The engine scales the colour of a lamp in the game's light draws, and the same way in the weights of the ray traced lamp shadows and in the lamp fog; nothing else is drawn. A lamp flickers when its colour is flame-coloured (red the strongest, green between 0.15 and 0.97 of red, blue at most 0.8 of red, a reach of at most 40 m) and the game kept its colour and reach unchanged for 1 s; the flicker then fades in over 1 s. A lamp whose colour or reach the game changes from frame to frame (the hunter's lantern flickers already) is left alone while the on-game strength is 0 (its lamp records stay exactly as the game made them), and one that starts to change loses an added flicker within 0.2 s. With the on-game strength above 0 such a lamp gets a flicker of its own on top of the game's, with a swing of 0.14 times the on-game strength, fading in over 1 s while the game changes it; once the game has kept it steady for 1 s that flicker fades out within 0.2 s and the steady lamps' flicker fades in over 1 s. A light draw whose lamp records would take more than 4096 bytes to copy turns the flicker off for the rest of the session. After the first 120 frames and then every 600 frames the log has a `Lamp flicker (r96):` line with the counts. Cost: no GPU pass; a light draw with a flickering lamp reads its lamp records from a copy in the stream buffer that the CPU writes from guest memory (an estimate of 0.2 to 1 ms of render thread time a frame with 600 to 1,500 light draws, not measured).

### Ambient particles (in development)

Dust and ash motes and a few embers in the air around the camera, drawn by the engine (not the game's own particles). Off unless `BB_PARTICLES` is above 0. These environment variables are read once when the game starts; they are for testing and are part of no look; no ini key yet. The motes are drawn after the upscaler (DLSS, FSR, XeSS or TAA) into the output-size frame, before the game's UI, so they need no motion vectors and stay sharp. Where: by default an output other than 1920x1080 is upscaled from the game's finished frame, and the motes are drawn right after that upscale; with `upscale_before_post=1` (or `BB_UPSCALE_EARLY=1`) it upscales the scene before the game's post-processing, the game finishes the frame at the output size, and the motes are drawn into the output-size copy of that finished frame the UI is drawn over (made at the first UI draw, or at the display pass of a frame without UI), only in a frame of the world the upscaler took. With a 1920x1080 output (the launcher then sets no render size, so neither place is reached), or with `BB_UPSCALER=none`, nothing is drawn. Where the wind shelter field (`BB_WIND=1` with `BB_WIND_SHELTER` above 0) says the camera is sheltered, as indoors, the air stands still: the motes only float slowly and dust over a dark picture fades (`BB_PARTICLES_STILL`, `BB_PARTICLES_DARK`); without the field it is one look indoors and outdoors (the engine has no other cheap test for an interior here); a mote takes its light from the picture behind it and from the lamps near it (`BB_PARTICLES_LAMPS`), so it shows where there is light and fades in the dark; flames throw embers.

### Cloud shadows (in development)

### Volumetric clouds (in development)

A layer of ray marched clouds in front of the painted sky. The game's sky is one painted dome whose clouds never move, so this draws real clouds between that dome and the world, only over the pixels where the sky shows (depth 1, or a view z beyond 500 m: the game's sky dome is drawn a little short of depth 1; `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_SKYZ` sets that distance). They are a slab of air 300 m thick whose base is 700 m above the world's y = 0, marched along each sky pixel's view ray in 12 steps at half the scene's resolution and laid over the sky with a bilinear filter that never takes a colour from, or puts one on, a pixel that is not sky. They are lit by the direction of the sun or moon that the engine already finds for the sun shadows (the effects that resolve the sun shadows must be on; without a light known the clouds have only ambient light) and tinted by the painted sky behind them (the dome's own colour with the game's fog on it, turned 55% toward its grey), so they sit in the painting: dark on the underside, with a bright forward-scattered rim toward the light. (r119d: the shade side and the lit side are coloured apart: the shade is the sky's tint turned 55% toward its grey and cooled, at a quarter of the ambient at the base up to its full value at the top; the lit light is the sky's tint turned 27% toward its grey, 3 times its brightness at the most near the light, weighed by how open the way toward the light is, with a faint floor for a dense core: dark undersides and bright rims against the painted sky.) A cloud is dense in its core (an opacity of at most 0.92) with a narrow edge band, so the clouds are separate masses and the painted sky shows through only at the thin edges and between them, and they fade out toward the horizon (r119i: by default a ray rising less than 12 degrees is not clouded and one above 30 degrees is in full, `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_HORIZON=0` gives the older fade, a ray's slope 0.05 to 0.22; and they sit behind the painting: where the painted dome has shapes of its own, a ridge, the rim of a painted cloud, the opacity is taken back, `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_PAINTED`, and a cloud's colour is at most 1.6 times the sky's brightness behind it, `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_GLOWMAX`) and beyond 5 to 10 km. The density is the cloud shadows' kind of field (`BB_CLOUD_SHADOWS`, r105b: the same three octaves of value noise) at the layer's own size and with its own edges (r119c: `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_SIZE`, 800 m for the biggest octave, so a cloud is some hundred metres to a kilometre across with clear sky between the masses; the shadows' 100 m cells averaged out over a sky ray into one grey blanket; edges smoothstep(0.51, 0.57, n) at cover 0.5, a band of 0.06 so an edge reads as an edge, about half of the sky covered). The layer drifts offsets of its own, so its clouds and the ground shadows no longer line up one for one. The heading and speed are the one wind's cloud heading and speed (times `BB_VOLUMETRIC_CLOUDS_SPEED`). While the cloud shadows are on the layer reads their clock itself; with them off it keeps a clock of its own made from the same variables (`BB_CLOUD_SHADOWS_SCALE`, `BB_CLOUDS_DIR`, `BB_WIND_DIR`, `BB_WIND_SPEED`, and the wind's direction with `BB_WIND=1`), so the heading and speed are the wind's `clouds` line in both cases. It is drawn at the upscaler's trigger, after the sky, the world and the drawn look and before the volumetric fog composite, the tonemap and the upscaler, so fog and haze lie over the clouds and the HUD is never touched; without the upscaler nothing is drawn (the log says so after 300 frames). ### Halation (in development)

The strength has a slider in the in-game menu (**Insert**, section Light glow and flicker, changes at once) and in the launcher (Graphics > Lighting).

Film halation: a soft red-orange glow that bleeds from the brightest small sources (lamps, candles, the torch flame, bright windows, the moon) into the dark around them. It is tighter and redder than the game's own bloom, which stays as it is. It is drawn into the scene before the upscaler, after the fog, at the scene's render size (1920x1080 for 4K output with DLSS Performance), so the HUD, which the game draws later on the upscaled picture, is never touched; without the upscaler (`BB_UPSCALER=none`) nothing is drawn. On by default at strength 1: the strength is the `lantern.ini` key `graphics_halation_strength`, 0 to 2, read every frame; `BB_HALATION`, when it is set and not empty, wins over the key (read once, at the first frame). At strength 0 nothing is drawn; the pass's pipeline, buffers and images are made at the first frame with a strength above 0 and kept until the game ends, also when the strength goes back to 0. The session log (with the Windows launcher, `last_session.log` in the `logs` folder) has a `Halation (r109a):` line with the strength and where it came from (setting or environment) at the first frame, and again after a change, at most once every 600 frames; lines that start with `Halation (r95` follow once the glow is drawn, among them one every 600 frames with the frames it drew on and the share of pixels over the threshold. `BB_HALATION_RADIUS`, `BB_HALATION_THRESHOLD` and `BB_HALATION_SHOW` are for testing and are part of no look; they are read once, at the first frame with a strength above 0; a missing or unreadable number takes the default, then the range is applied.

| Variable | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `graphics_halation_strength` | `lantern.ini` key: the strength, 0 to 2, read every frame, with the glow `BB_HALATION` describes; `BB_HALATION` wins over it when set. A value outside the range is the nearest end, one that is not a finite number is `1`, text that is no number is `0` | `1` |

### Heat haze (in development)

A shimmer of rising warm air above flames: the hunter's torch, candles, lanterns, the flames of lamps and bonfires. The game itself moves no pixel behind a flame (its fire is drawn as sprites that read no copy of the picture), so this adds the haze and doubles nothing. Off by default. The strength is the `lantern.ini` key `graphics_heat_haze_strength`, 0 to 2, read every frame, with a slider in the in-game menu (**Insert**, section Light glow and flicker, changes at once) and in the launcher (Graphics > Lighting); 0, the default, is off, 1 is the look as designed. `BB_HEAT_HAZE`, when it is set and not empty, wins over the key (read once, at the first frame). The session log has a `Heat haze (r97e):` line with the strength and where it came from (setting or environment) at the first frame that reaches the place before the upscaler, and again after a change, at most once every 600 frames. The other variables are read once when the game starts; they are for testing.

### Blood sheen (in development)

The blood pool beside a body (the graveyard) looks dry: the game's lamps (the hunter's lantern, torches, street lamps) light it as they light dry ground. The pool is in the game's G-buffer: the r100c probe found that its albedo is written by a G-buffer draw of the ground's own shader pair (`c829ee58:dbb222eb`), not by a draw after the lighting. With `BB_BLOOD_SHEEN` the engine gives the picked pixels a wet film in the game's G-buffer before the game's lights run (gloss set toward 0.86 for every wet pixel (the ground's own gloss may go down, so the pool's gloss is uniform), the specular colour's square root raised toward 0.45 (f0 0.20 before the tint), by the strength, and tinted by the blood: its red, green and blue are multiplied by 0.85, 0.35 and 0.30 (a deep red: f0 red 0.146, green 0.025, blue 0.018 at strength 1), so the moon gives a dark red highlight and a torch a warm red-orange one; the red is never lowered, the green and blue go down to the tinted value on the wet pixels; the pool's rim (the gap-fill pixels that are not dark red themselves) gets at most 0.35 of that tint and gloss, scaled again by its own fill weight (squared falloff; BLOOD_SHEEN_FILL_SPEC 0.35, rising smoothly to the full value where 80-100% of the ring taps are dark red) so the gravel at the edge reads as a soft wet edge without red sparkle; the target the game keeps its lighting record in is not touched; the pixel's normal also goes all the way, times the strength (at most 1), to world up inside the pool (the share of 24 taps around the pixel, three rings of 8 at 6, 14 and 28 pixels out at 1080p, whose albedo is dark red is at least 0.65; the weight falls to 0.3 where it is 0.25 or less, at the pool's rim, so the rim follows the stones; with a draw key and no albedo rule there is no rim), weighted by how much the pixel's own normal faces up (none below 0.35, all above 0.6), so the film is a level surface and its highlights are smooth sheets, not the speckle of the cobbles' bumps; the weight comes from the pixel and fixed taps of the albedo, which this pass does not write, so no block shows in it; 24 texel fetches for each picked pixel; a ground pixel of the named draw whose own albedo is not dark red (a stone or a crack the mask leaves out) is made wet and levelled too when at least 2 of its 8 taps at 14 pixels are dark red and the share of its 24 taps is 0.5 to 0.8 (weight smoothstep 0.5 to 0.8, times the up weight of its normal, so walls and sides are left alone; it needs the normal target; 8 fetches for each such pixel, 24 more where the first 8 pass), so the ripple of the albedo mask no longer breaks the highlight; use it with a named draw: with the albedo rule alone an up-facing surface of another object inside a pool could be filled too; the count of filled pixels is the wet count minus the albedo rule count of the log's recent-frame line), so the game's sun or moon (shadowed as the game shadows it, ray traced when the ray traced sun is on) and every lamp of its light grid (each with the engine's ray traced lamp shadow when lamp shadows are on) put a highlight on them through the game's own lighting, and only where light reaches them. Which pixels are picked is set by two knobs, so one build can try both readings of the probe: `BB_BLOOD_SHEEN_DRAW` names G-buffer draws (a shader pair, or a pair and an index count for one mesh such as a decal), and every pixel such a draw wrote last in its G-buffer pass is picked; `BB_BLOOD_SHEEN_RULE=albedo` picks only pixels whose albedo is dark red (with a named draw: of that draw's pixels; without one: anywhere in the scene). Nothing is picked unless one of the two is set (no draw is named by default). Not in it: reflections, bounced light and fog. The film is not laid while a lamps-apart batch is still open at the first light draw after the G-buffer pass (counted in the log), nor in a pass with a named draw where no named draw was drawn, nor in a menu's 3D preview, nor in a G-buffer pass of another camera (a planar reflection). These environment variables are read once when the game starts; they are for testing, no ini key yet. When on, the session log has lines starting with `Blood sheen (r100d)`: the knobs at the start (the draws named, the rule and its numbers, and a warning when nothing can be picked), the first wet-ground pass, the first unfit G-buffer target, and every 600 frames the frames with named draws, the named draws (and those over the cap of 32 a pass), the wet-ground and show passes, the reasons for leaving one out, and a recent frame's counts: pixels the named draws changed, of those still theirs at the first light draw and drawn over later, pixels that passed the albedo rule, pixels made wet. A second line (`Blood sheen (r100e)`) says what those pixels had before the film (their mean gloss and specular root, and how many were already at the film's gloss: a film at or below the ground's own values changes nothing) and how many wet passes flattened the normal. After the game's opaque lighting, the picked pixels of the lit scene are also dimmed and reddened where they are dark (luminance below 0.1: multiplied by 0.7 red and 0.4 green and blue, times `BB_BLOOD_SHEEN_DARKEN` and the strength up to 1; none above luminance 0.6, so highlights stay), because a wet film darkens what is under it and the game's albedo cannot be written; the test view `show` never dims.

### Footprints (in development)

After the hunter walks out of water, his feet leave wet prints on the ground that fade out over 30 seconds: darker and glossier than the ground around them, so the game's own sun or moon, its lamps and its reflections light them. Off unless `BB_FOOTPRINTS` is set. The engine does not see the game's animation, so the steps come from the hunter's path: once a frame it reads his position from the game, and each time he has gone 0.75 m across since the last print, a print is placed at his position, 0.11 m to the left or right of his path in turn, pointing along it (a boot's sole about 0.28 m long and 0.11 m wide at the ball: a heel block, a narrow waist and a ball). A move of more than 2 m across or 1 m up or down in one frame (a warp, a load, a fall) starts the path anew. The feet are wet while his position lies more than 3 cm under the water plane of a frame the game draws with a planar water reflection (the sewers), or while the ground around his feet holds a puddle: of the up-facing pixels within 0.45 m of him and 8 cm of his height (read back from the GPU a few frames late), at least 24, and at least 30% of them with a gloss of 0.88 or more. No print is placed while he stands in the water. Each print's strength is what the feet carry, and the first 3 prints leave the whole of it, then each step leaves 0.75 of it; below 0.06 they are dry (so 12 prints after wading out: 3 full, then 9 fading). The coat is picked up from the ground under the feet. Blood: of the up-facing ground pixels within 25 cm of his position and 8 cm of his height (counted by the pass before any print is laid), at least 24 pixels and at least 25% of them are dark red by the albedo rule (linear albedo: red 0.01 to 0.12, and red at least 2.5 times the green and 2.5 times the blue; the graveyard pool's band averages 0.021 0.006 0.005, a bright red 0.3 0.02 0.015 is not blood); the feet then carry blood at a strength of that share over 50% (at most 1), the first 4 prints leave the whole strength and each step after keeps 0.75 of it (0.75, 0.56, 0.42 for a full coat), and the feet are dry (below 0.06) after 13 prints (4 full, then 9 fading). Water, blood and a puddle are tried in that order and the newest pickup wins: a new source replaces the coat's kind and strength; a wet coat leaves 12 prints (3 full, then 9 fading). The rule lives in `blood_albedo.h` over `blood_albedo_layout.h` (its numbers, which shaders include; the GLSL twin is `IsBloodAlbedo` in `footprints.comp`), with a unit test. The trail rules: a coat's trail is its first 2 prints at the whole strength and then a smoothstep taper to nothing over 14 prints (wet) or 16 (blood), the last print about 1% of the coat, under the shader's 1/64 limit in all but a hair, so a trail ends in nothing instead of stopping at a visible print; blood is picked up from 15% of the pickup disc (was 25%: the graveyard pool's own albedo, 0.031 0.015 0.014, is blood by the rule over only about 30% of it, so the pickup flickered at the pool's edge and left a coat of half strength) and held while the feet stand in it down to 8%, a puddle is picked up as before (30% of the ground band glossy) and held down to 15%, a blood coat's strength is its share over 30% (was 50%) and the strongest share of one visit is kept, standing in a source starts the trail over; and a new print fades in over 0.30 s (smoothstep) before it fades out over 30 s. Cost: a few multiplies a frame on the CPU, nothing on the GPU. In a build with the test tools' live knobs (`BB_LIVE_KNOBS_FILE`) it is asked again every frame | on |
| `BB_FOOTPRINTS_LOOK` | Only with `BB_FOOTPRINTS` on (r111k). `0`: r111j's look. Unset, empty or anything else: on. Water and blood are told apart: a wet print darkens the lit pixel 65% in the post stamp (was 55%) and the albedo 70% in the G-buffer (was 65%), raises a dull ground's gloss toward 0.85 in the G-buffer (never lowers it; the specular colour is not touched, as r111g left it, so a glossy floor shows no pale shape) and, on near-black ground where a darkening cannot show, is lifted to a faint cool grey (luminance floor 0.007, hue 0.88 1.02 1.11, by the print's weight; lit ground above the floor is not touched); a blood print keeps its dark red (the stone's own red at 0.25 of its luminance) and has r111j's blood sheen floor always on (as `BB_FOOTPRINTS_SHEEN=1`, 0.012 in the red tint; a larger `BB_FOOTPRINTS_SHEEN` still wins), so on near-black stone a blood print is a faint red sole and a wet one a fainter grey-blue sole: dark, cool and glossy against dark red. The knob travels in the push words `hunter.w` (G-buffer pass) and `hunter.y` (post stamp). Cost: a few ALU operations on the pixels a print stamps and one gloss write on wet G-buffer pixels; no memory. In a build with the test tools' live knobs (`BB_LIVE_KNOBS_FILE`) it is asked again every frame, so one running game can show the same view with and without it | on |
